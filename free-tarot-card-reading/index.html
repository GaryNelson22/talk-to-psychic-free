    From shuffle to conversation

    1. Shuffle firstThink of what you would like to talk through and shuffle the deck. Add a question if it helps.
    2. Choose sixCards wait face down. Tap the six you like the look of, and tap one twice to swap it out.
    3. Read, then chatEach card flips with a short note in its position. Take what resonates, and bring any open questions to a conversation if you choose.

    The six positions

    1. How you see yourself now. The picture of yourself you are carrying into this question.
    2. What you truly want. The wish beneath the practical goals.
    3. What you fear. The worry that quietly shapes your choices.
    4. What works in your favour. A strength or support you can lean on.
    5. What stands in your way. The obstacle, whether outside you or inside.
    6. Where this is heading. The direction of travel if you carry on as you are.

    Card by card: what to talk about

    If you plan to chat with someone about your spread, it helps to know what each card is about. Here is a quick summary of the 22 Major Arcana, plus the kind of question each one can open up.

    The Fool

    Fresh starts · Open-hearted trust · Spontaneity · Innocence

    Conversation starter: what new thing are you hesitating to begin? The Fool stands for beginnings and a leap of trust. Bring your plans, however unfinished, and talk about which part is exciting and which part needs a bit of caution.

    The Magician

    Focused will · Skill and resourcefulness · Manifestation · Clear intention

    Conversation starter: what resources do you already have? The Magician says your skills and ideas are enough to begin. It makes a good opener for talking about goals, and how to turn a wish into a specific first move.

    The High Priestess

    Intuition · Hidden knowledge · Stillness · Inner voice

    Conversation starter: what do you sense but cannot explain? The High Priestess concerns intuition and unspoken knowledge. It is useful for discussing hunches, hidden factors and the value of waiting before you decide. Share whatever feels half-formed; it counts.

    The Empress

    Abundance · Nurturing · Creativity · Sensual pleasure

    Conversation starter: what needs nurturing? The Empress speaks of creativity, comfort and growth. Talk through how you are caring for a project or relationship, and whether you give yourself the same attention you offer to others.

    The Emperor

    Structure · Authority · Stability · Leadership

    Conversation starter: where do you need more structure? The Emperor is about order, planning and firm boundaries. It opens a conversation about responsibility, leadership and whether your current rules help or simply hold you back.

    The Hierophant

    Tradition · Shared beliefs · Mentorship · Institutions

    Conversation starter: whose advice do you trust? The Hierophant refers to traditions, teachers and shared beliefs. It is a good prompt for discussing mentors, expectations from family or community, and which conventions deserve keeping. Say which rules you would keep and which you would drop.

    The Lovers

    Choice and alignment · Deep connection · Shared values · Union

    Conversation starter: which choice matches your values? The Lovers is about connection and decisions made honestly. It works for relationship topics, though it equally suits any fork in the road where integrity is the deciding factor.

    The Chariot

    Determination · Self-mastery · Forward momentum · Victory through effort

    Conversation starter: where are you heading, and what is pulling you off course? The Chariot signals determination and self-control. Use it to talk about goals, momentum and how to hold competing priorities in one direction.

    Strength

    Quiet courage · Compassion · Patience · Inner strength

    Conversation starter: what takes quiet courage right now? Strength is about patience, compassion and steady resolve. It invites you to discuss how you handle fear or anger, and what a gentler response to a hard situation might be.

    The Hermit

    Solitude · Inner guidance · Reflection · Wisdom

    Conversation starter: what would you hear if everything went quiet? The Hermit encourages solitude and self-guidance. It is handy for discussing whether you need time alone, and what answers your own judgement already holds. Mention how much quiet time you currently get.

    Wheel of Fortune

    Cycles and change · Turning point · Fortune · Fate and timing

    Conversation starter: what is changing around you? The Wheel represents cycles, luck and turning points. Talk about which shifts you can influence, which you cannot, and how to remain flexible while circumstances rotate. Bring examples of recent shifts, large or small.

    Justice

    Fairness and balance · Accountability · Clear-eyed truth · Cause and effect

    Conversation starter: what would an honest assessment say? Justice points to fairness, accountability and consequences. It gives you a framework for discussing past decisions, current commitments and whether the balance of give and take feels right.

    The Hanged Man

    Pause and surrender · New perspective · Letting go of control · Voluntary sacrifice

    Conversation starter: what happens if you stop pushing? The Hanged Man is about pausing and shifting perspective. It suits topics where you feel stuck, and opens room to ask what you might see from a different angle.

    Death

    Endings and transformation · Letting go · Clearing the old · Necessary change

    Conversation starter: what is ready to end? Death symbolises transformation, not literal loss. Use it to talk about habits, roles or relationships that have run their course, and what you hope will take their place.

    Temperance

    Balance and moderation · Patience · Blending opposites · Healing and harmony

    Conversation starter: where do you need balance? Temperance is moderation, patience and gradual healing. It leads naturally to talking about stress, competing demands, and small adjustments that could make your days feel steadier. Mention which areas feel lopsided at the moment.

    The Devil

    Attachment and bondage · Temptation · Unhealthy patterns · Shadow side

    Conversation starter: what has a grip on you? The Devil refers to attachments, temptations and unhelpful patterns. It is a candid way to discuss habits or dependencies, with the reassurance that the chains in the image are loose.

    The Tower

    Sudden upheaval · Collapse of false structures · Revelation · Shock

    Conversation starter: what has recently been shaken? The Tower represents sudden change and truths coming out. Use it to talk through an upheaval, what it revealed, and how to rebuild on firmer ground once the shock settles.

    The Star

    Hope and renewal · Healing · Inspiration · Serenity

    Conversation starter: where do you see hope? The Star brings calm, healing and renewal after hardship. It is a gentle opener for discussing recovery, what has helped so far, and the small signs that things are improving.

    The Moon

    Illusion and uncertainty · Intuition · Subconscious fears · Dreams

    Conversation starter: which worries are real? The Moon deals with illusion, dreams and half-seen fears. It helps you separate what you know from what you imagine, and decide what to verify before making any big decision.

    The Sun

    Joy and vitality · Clarity and success · Warmth · Confidence

    Conversation starter: what is going well? The Sun is about joy, clarity and success. It is a pleasant card to discuss, since it invites you to name your wins, appreciate them, and consider how to keep that energy going.

    Judgement

    Awakening and calling · Reckoning · Renewal · Self-forgiveness

    Conversation starter: what is calling you forward? Judgement asks for honest self-review and forgiveness. It opens discussion about leaving old patterns behind, and answering the sense that you are meant for something different. Talk about what you would like to forgive.

    The World

    Completion · Wholeness · Fulfillment · Integration

    Conversation starter: what have you completed? The World signals a cycle coming to a satisfying finish. It is ideal for reflecting on how far you have come, and what the next stage might look like.

    Free tarot reading FAQ

    Do I have to talk to anyone after the reading?

    No. The reading is complete on its own, and the button to speak with a live psychic is entirely optional. Plenty of visitors shuffle, read their spread, and close the page. Nothing here pressures you to take another step or share anything personal. Many visitors like to run the spread first, jot down a couple of themes, and only then decide whether a conversation sounds useful for them.

    What can I ask the cards before a chat?

    Open questions work best, such as what do I need to understand about this decision, or what is holding me back. They give you themes to explore in conversation. Questions that demand a fixed prediction usually produce vaguer answers, so aim for clarity instead of certainty. If you do speak with someone later, bringing a card or two as talking points can give the conversation a helpful starting structure and save time.

    Is my question visible to anyone?

    It is not. Your text stays inside this page in your browser and returns only in your own result. It is not saved or sent elsewhere, and refreshing the tab erases it, so you can try as many shuffles as you want. There is also nothing to log in to, which means you can try several different questions in a row without leaving a trail anywhere on the page.

    How should I treat what the cards say?

    Use it as food for thought. A spread can highlight feelings and options, but it cannot decide for you or forecast fixed events. It is provided for entertainment and self-reflection, so keep serious health, legal and financial choices with qualified experts. Remember that a spread offers prompts, not instructions, and that a good conversation, with a friend or a professional, is often where those prompts become useful.
